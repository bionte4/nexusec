# Closing the Assessment–Operations Gap: NexusSec, a Compliance-Driven Orchestration Platform for Continuous Vulnerability Management

# 1. Introduction

The digital attack surface of modern organizations continues to expand as enterprises adopt hybrid cloud workloads, distributed web applications, and interconnected operational systems. Concurrently, the volume and sophistication of cyber threats have increased, with vulnerability disclosure pipelines and automated scanning tooling generating large quantities of security signals that must be triaged under time pressure. Peer-reviewed security research shows that static severity scores alone are poor predictors of real-world exploit development, motivating data-driven exploitability and prioritization models [1], [2]. Empirical SOC studies further document extreme false-positive and “benign trigger” volumes that drive analyst fatigue [15], [16]. At the same time, enterprise networks face advanced, long-lived adversary behaviors that challenge purely reactive detection, reinforcing the need for proactive SOC-oriented tooling and threat-informed operations [3]. Free international technical books and frameworks—such as NIST CSF 2.0 and incident-response guidance [18], [19]—likewise emphasize continuous risk management rather than ad-hoc tooling. In this environment, Vulnerability Assessment (VA), Penetration Testing (PT), and Security Operations Center (SOC) functions can no longer operate as loosely coupled, document-centric processes; they require platforms that can orchestrate evidence collection, normalize findings, map controls to regulatory obligations, and accelerate remediation decisions.

In practice, many SOC and AppSec workflows remain heavily manual. Analysts frequently invoke specialized third-party scanners—such as network discovery and service fingerprinting tools (e.g., Nmap) and template-driven vulnerability scanners (e.g., Nuclei)—then copy intermediate outputs into tickets, spreadsheets, or disconnected dashboards. Surveys of security orchestration and automation highlight that many SOAR-style approaches remain incomplete or layer-specific rather than end-to-end assessment-to-operations pipelines [4], while hands-on evaluations of commercial SOAR tools expose configuration and usability challenges in real SOCs [17]. Closed-loop automation research continues to seek tighter coupling between detection, policy, and enforcement [5]. Tool fragmentation introduces inconsistent severity interpretation across scanners, duplicated effort when correlating findings to assets, slow hand-offs between assessment and remediation owners, and limited auditability of who changed finding status and why. False-positive noise further consumes analyst time; recent deep-learning and transformer-based studies specifically target vulnerability-scanner and static-analysis false-alarm reduction [6], [7], consistent with qualitative SOC evidence on alarm validation burden [15].

Regulatory and assurance obligations intensify these challenges. Frameworks and standards such as ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (information security management and Annex A controls), operationalized in practice via free NIST SP 800-53 / OLIR crosswalks [12], [20], the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) v4.0—particularly continuous vulnerability management and testing expectations associated with Requirement 11 [13]—and the European Union General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) with respect to security of processing of personal data [14] impose expectations for demonstrable, continuous compliance rather than point-in-time checklist exercises. Complementary free NIST risk, patch, and testing guidance [49]–[52] and OWASP application-security taxonomies [53], [54] further shape how organizations operationalize vulnerability and secure-development controls. Security leaders therefore need not only detection capability, but also traceable mapping from each vulnerability to relevant control references, evidence of remediation lifecycle (open, in progress, false positive, resolved), and reporting suitable for auditors and risk committees. Without integrated compliance metadata in the operational pipeline, mapping is typically performed retrospectively and inconsistently, reducing the credibility of assurance narratives.

**Research gap.** Despite the maturity of individual scanners, ticketing systems, and general-purpose security platforms, a critical integration gap remains. Existing approaches commonly exhibit one or more of the following limitations: (i) they lack *first-class, per-finding compliance mapping* that connects normalized vulnerabilities to ISO 27001, PCI-DSS, and GDPR-oriented risk tags within the same data model used by SOC workflows [12]–[14]; (ii) they provide limited *operational* AI-driven remediation guidance for SOC findings—whereas leading software-engineering research on automated vulnerability repair with Transformers/LLMs primarily targets source-code patches [8]–[10]; and (iii) they do not offer a *unified asynchronous orchestration framework* that safely executes third-party tools in isolated workers, normalizes heterogeneous outputs into a single schema, and exposes multi-tenant, API-first SOC operations (including triage assistants and false-positive analysis) without blocking interactive services [4], [5]. Recent open-access work on RAG-driven SOC copilots demonstrates generative AI value for incident guidance over SIEM context [11], yet open, research-oriented architectures that combine secure-by-design tool wrapping, unified multi-scanner normalization, compliance tagging, exploit-aware prioritization signals [1], [2], and LLM-assisted remediation/ChatOps in one asynchronous VA/PT–SOC platform remain underrepresented.

To address this gap, this paper presents **NexusSec**, a compliance-driven VA/PT and SOC orchestration platform. NexusSec is designed around modular, microservices-ready components: an asynchronous FastAPI core for authentication, multi-tenancy, asset inventory, scan lifecycle, and vulnerability management; Redis-backed Celery workers for long-running scan execution and enrichment jobs; Docker-isolated execution for third-party scanners; a unified normalization layer that converts multi-tool outputs into a standardized finding schema enriched with CWE/CVSS, MITRE ATT&CK, and compliance metadata; and AI services for remediation patch generation (via OpenAI-compatible LLM providers), false-positive analysis, and retrieval-augmented SOC ChatOps over live PostgreSQL context, in the spirit of RAG-enabled SOC assistants [11]. The platform further incorporates threat-intelligence enrichment (e.g., CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities and NVD-oriented signals) to support risk-based prioritization consistent with exploit-prediction research [1], [2].

**Contributions and research objectives.** The main contributions of this work are as follows:

1. **Unified orchestration architecture** — We design and implement an asynchronous hybrid architecture that orchestrates VA/PT scanning, normalization, SOC remediation tracking, and reporting behind a secure multi-tenant API, with background workers isolating long-running and potentially unsafe tool execution from the request path [4], [5].

2. **Normalized finding model with compliance mapping** — We define a single vulnerability schema that consolidates multi-scanner evidence and attaches extensible compliance metadata (ISO 27001, PCI-DSS, GDPR) [12]–[14] alongside standard taxonomies (CWE, CVSS, MITRE ATT&CK), enabling auditor-oriented reporting without a separate offline mapping exercise.

3. **AI-assisted SOC decision support** — We integrate LLM-backed services for secure remediation guidance and patch sketches informed by automated vulnerability-repair research [8]–[10], structured false-positive confidence scoring for analyst triage [6], [7], and a lightweight RAG ChatOps endpoint that answers natural-language SOC queries using retrieved assets, scans, and vulnerabilities [11].

4. **Operational security and observability foundations** — We embed secure-by-design practices (strict RBAC, audit trails, avoidance of unsafe shell interpolation for tool wrappers), multi-organization tenancy, health/metrics exposure, and scheduled threat-intel synchronization suitable for continuous operations and exploit-aware prioritization [1]–[3], aligned with NIST CSF / incident-response and control catalogs [18]–[20].

The research objectives guiding the evaluation of NexusSec are: (a) to demonstrate that asynchronous orchestration can sustain concurrent scan workloads while preserving API responsiveness; (b) to show that a unified normalization pipeline can reliably ingest heterogeneous scanner outputs into a consistent schema; and (c) to assess whether AI-assisted remediation and triage features produce actionable, security-conscious guidance that reduces analyst effort relative to purely manual SOC workflows. Collectively, these objectives position NexusSec as a reproducible reference architecture for organizations seeking to unify vulnerability assessment, SOC operations, and continuous compliance evidence in a single platform.
